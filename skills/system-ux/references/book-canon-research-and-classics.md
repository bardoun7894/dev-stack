## Part III — UX Research & Psychological Constraints (IxDF Books 16–20)

### 16. *The User Experience Team of One* — Leah Buley (2nd Ed. with Joe Natoli)
- **Core Thesis:** Constraints are a designer's friend. A solo practitioner or small dev team can achieve high-impact UX using lightweight, high-leverage methods ("If you can only do one of these, do X").
- **Key Frameworks:** **27 Modular Methods across 5 Phases** — Planning (UX Questionnaire, Listening Tour, Project Brief), Research (Learning Plan, Guerrilla Research, Proto-Personas, Heuristic Markup, Comparative Assessment), Design (Design Principles, Sketchboards, Task Flows, Wireframes), Testing (Paper/Interactive Prototypes, **Black Hat Session**, Quick-and-Dirty Usability Test, **Five-Second Test**, UX Health Check), Evangelism (Mini Case Studies, Captive UX).
- **System-App Rule:** When time/budget is tight, always run at least: Proto-Personas + Task Flows + Heuristic Markup + 5-Second Test + Black Hat critique before shipping.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stages 1, 7.

### 17. *Just Enough Research* — Erika Hall
- **Core Thesis:** You can optimize everything and still fail if you optimize the wrong thing. Match the right lightweight research method to the specific question you need answered.
- **Key Frameworks:** **4 Research Purposes** (Generative, Descriptive, Evaluative, Causal); Organizational (stakeholder + frontline support interviews), User (Contextual Inquiry & Ethnography — *never focus groups*), Competitive & Brand Audits, Evaluative (Heuristic Analysis + Usability Testing), Collaborative Affinity Analysis, Mental Models, Scenarios; Surveys warned as "most dangerous" if biased; Quant/Analytics used for optimization *after* Qual answers *Why*.
- **System-App Rule:** Interview frontline operators/support first; observe real workflows rather than trusting hypothetical feature requests; never replace usability testing with opinion surveys.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stages 1, 7.

### 18. *Think Like a UX Researcher* — David Travis & Philip Hodgson
- **Core Thesis:** Great design doesn't live in rockstar designers—it lives in users' heads. All UX research answers **Two Questions**: (1) Who are users and what are they trying to do? (*Field Research*) and (2) Can they use what we designed to solve it? (*Usability Testing*).
- **Key Frameworks:** Pilot studies ("dress rehearsals"), **Episodic Interviews** (specific past stories, not future opinions), **UX Detective Method** (separate raw facts from interpretation → hypotheses), **Strength of Evidence** triangulation, **Usability Severity Decision Tree** (Impact × Users Affected × Recurrence), **Information Radiators** (single-glance visual summaries instead of unread reports), Design Studio workshops.
- **System-App Rule:** Prioritize UX defects objectively via the Severity Decision Tree; ask for concrete past incidents (*"Walk me through the last time you processed an invoice"*), never *"Would you use feature X?"*.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stages 1, 7.

### 19. *Bottlenecks: Aligning UX Design with User Psychology* — David C. Evans, Ph.D.
- **Core Thesis:** Features and products behave like memes competing to survive a sequential funnel of **7 innate psychological bottlenecks**. Failing an early bottleneck (Attention/Perception/Memory) cannot be rescued by later motivation or marketing tricks.
- **Key Frameworks:** **The 7 Psychological Bottlenecks Funnel:**
  1. **Attention:** Foveal acuity, Task-Positive vs. Task-Negative orientation, exclusive focus.
  2. **Perception:** Instant Gestalt meaning, depth hierarchy, natural motion cues.
  3. **Memory:** Fragile working memory, **Signal Detection Theory** (distinguish real signals from noise to cut misses & false alarms), elaborative long-term encoding.
  4. **Disposition:** Personality, life/career stage, core human needs (safety, mastery, status), controlled fun.
  5. **Motivation:** Reinforcement schedules, **Escalating Commitment** (small ask first, big ask later), resolving **Approach–Avoidance Conflict** at decision points.
  6. **Social Influence:** Social capital, moderating group polarization, authentic credibility.
  7. **Receptivity:** Earned only after Bottlenecks 1–6 pass.
- **System-App Rule:** Audit core workflows top-down through Bottlenecks 1→5; separate critical alerts (signal) from routine telemetry (noise); use escalating commitment in onboarding.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stage 6.

### 20. *User Research: Improve Product and Service Design* — Stephanie Marsh
- **Core Thesis:** Operationalizing UX research end-to-end—ethically, inclusively, and systematically—turns raw observations into service and product improvements.
- **Key Frameworks:** 4-Phase Lifecycle (Fundamentals/Ethics/GDPR/Consent → Method Selection & Mixing: Usability, Content Testing, Card Sorting, Tree Testing, Diary Studies, Contextual Inquiry, A/B → Analysis: Thematic Analysis, Atomic Research, Mental Models → UX Storytelling & Actionable Recommendations).
- **System-App Rule:** Validate complex system navigation trees via **Card Sorting** (to create) and **Tree Testing** (to verify findability without visual crutches); maintain ethical data consent.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stages 1, 3, 7.

## Part IV — Free Ebooks & The Interaction Design Bible (IxDF Books 21–23)

### 21. *The Basics of User Experience Design* — Interaction Design Foundation (IxDF)
- **Core Thesis:** Holistic UX is governed by **Peter Morville's UX Honeycomb (7 Factors)**: *Useful, Usable, Findable, Credible, Desirable, Accessible, Valuable*.
- **Key Frameworks:** Design Thinking 5 Stages (Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test), 7 UX Factors, Usability basics, User Interviews, Mobile Web UX guidelines, Information Visualization fundamentals.
- **System-App Rule:** Check every deliverable against all 7 UX Factors—an app that is Usable but not Findable or Credible still fails.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stages 1, 2, 7.

### 22. *Bright Ideas for User Experience Designers* — Userfocus (David Travis, Philip Hodgson, Ritch Macefield)
- **Core Thesis:** Practical, high-craft habits—rapid paper prototyping, crisp error communication, visual CRAP alignment, accessible alt-text, and a shared UX Vision—separate amateur UIs from trustworthy systems.
- **Key Frameworks:** CRAP visual principles (Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, Proximity), 7 myths of paper prototyping, **Communicating Errors** (clear, polite, specific, actionable), writing task-centered support/help, creating & publicizing a UX Vision, 5 kinds of `alt` text, IA Card Games.
- **System-App Rule:** Write every error message, empty state, and inline help text to Userfocus's clarity standard; define a one-sentence UX Vision in Stage 1.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stages 1, 5, 6.

### 23. *About Face: The Essentials of Interaction Design* — Alan Cooper, Robert Reimann, David Cronin & Christopher Noessel
- **Core Thesis:** Software fails when it forces humans to conform to the **implementation model**. Align the **represented model** to users' **mental models** and **goals** via **Goal-Directed Design (GDD)**.
- **Key Frameworks:**
  - **6 Phases of GDD:** Research → Modeling (Personas) → Requirements (Context Scenarios) → Framework Definition → Refinement → Engineering Support.
  - **Goals vs. Tasks:** Goals are enduring (*why*); tasks are transient (*how*). Design for **perpetual intermediates** (most users stay intermediate forever—don't cripple them with welded-on training wheels or optimize only for gurus).
  - **Application Posture:** **Sovereign** (primary full-screen work), **Transient** (brief single-purpose utility), **Daemonic** (invisible background process), *Parasitic* (persistent companion).
  - **Excise Eradication:** Eliminate Navigation excise, GUI/Visual excise, Modal interruption ("stopping the proceedings with idiocy"), and welded-on training wheels; **Inflect** the UI by frequency.
  - **Idioms vs. Metaphors:** Prefer learn-once **Idioms** over brittle physical metaphors.
  - **4 Ps:** Principles, Patterns, Processes, and Practice.
- **System-App Rule:** Declare Application Posture before layout; design for perpetual intermediates; aggressively purge excise; prefer modeless undo over blocking dialogs.
- **Pipeline Stage:** Stages 1, 2, 4, 5.

## Part V — 7 Complementary UX Specialty Classics for System Applications

### 24. *The Elements of User Experience* — Jesse James Garrett
- **Core Framework:** **5 Stacked Planes** from abstract to concrete, split across *Functionality (Software)* and *Information (Hypertext)*:
  1. **Strategy** (Product Objectives + User Needs)
  2. **Scope** (Functional Specs + Content Requirements)
  3. **Structure** (Interaction Design + Information Architecture)
  4. **Skeleton** (Interface + Navigation + Information Design)
  5. **Surface** (Sensory Design)
- **System-App Rule:** Resolve lower planes before debating higher planes—never fix a broken Structure or Scope problem by tweaking Surface colors.

### 25. *Information Architecture: For the Web and Beyond* (4th Ed.) — Louis Rosenfeld, Peter Morville & Jorge Arango
- **Core Framework:** IA sits at the intersection of **Users, Content, and Context**, built from **4 Visible Systems** (**Organization, Labeling, Navigation, Search**) backed by **Invisible Metadata** (thesauri, controlled vocabularies, taxonomies).
- **System-App Rule:** Define controlled domain terminology once in a glossary so labels, breadcrumbs, search facets, and table headers never contradict each other.

### 26. *Laws of UX* (2nd Ed.) — Jon Yablonski
- **Core Framework:** **10 Psychological Laws** operationalized for software: **Jakob's Law** (familiar patterns), **Fitts's Law** (target size/distance), **Hick's Law** (choice latency), **Miller's Law** (chunking), **Postel's Law** (forgiving input, strict output), **Peak–End Rule** (optimize peak & closure), **Aesthetic–Usability Effect** (polish creates tolerance/trust), **Von Restorff Effect** (isolate the primary CTA), **Tesler's Law** (absorb irreducible complexity into the system), **Doherty Threshold** (`< 400ms` interaction pace).
- **System-App Rule:** Enforce `< 400ms` perceived responsiveness, forgiving input parsing, single isolated primary CTA per view, and deliberate Peak–End closure.

### 27. *Web Form Design: Filling in the Blanks* — Luke Wroblewski
- **Core Framework:** **4 Form Principles:** (1) Minimize the Pain (cut fields, smart defaults, forgiving inputs), (2) Illuminate a Path to Completion (single-column vertical flow, logical grouping, clear primary vs. secondary action hierarchy), (3) Consider Context (frequent vs. rare, familiar vs. foreign), (4) Consistent Communication (unified inline validation, help, and error voice).
- **System-App Rule:** Default to single-column top-aligned forms; never hide labels solely inside disappearing placeholders; match input width to expected data length.

### 28. *Inclusive Design Patterns* & *Inclusive Design Principles* — Heydon Pickering (et al.)
- **Core Framework:** Accessibility baked into semantic HTML & component architecture for permanent, temporary, and situational constraints. **7 Principles:** Comparable Experience, Consider Situation, Be Consistent, Give Control, Offer Choice, Prioritize Content, Add Value.
- **System-App Rule:** Use native semantic elements first (Follow the First Rule of ARIA), guarantee full keyboard operation & visible focus rings, never rely on color alone, and support RTL/zoom natively.

### 29. *Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design* — Ben Shneiderman
- **Core Framework:** (1) Strive for consistency, (2) Seek universal usability & shortcuts for frequent users, (3) Offer informative feedback, (4) Design dialogs to yield closure, (5) Prevent errors & offer simple handling, (6) Permit easy reversal of actions (Undo), (7) Keep internal locus of control (user initiates, system obeys), (8) Reduce short-term memory load.
- **System-App Rule:** Every multi-step workflow must end with explicit closure; every sovereign workspace must provide keyboard accelerators and easy Undo.

### 30. *10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design* — Jakob Nielsen
- **Core Framework:** (1) Visibility of system status, (2) Match between system and real world, (3) User control and freedom (emergency exits), (4) Consistency and standards, (5) Error prevention, (6) Recognition rather than recall, (7) Flexibility and efficiency of use, (8) Aesthetic and minimalist design, (9) Recognize/diagnose/recover from errors, (10) Task-focused help and documentation.
- **System-App Rule:** Primary rapid heuristic checklist for Mode C UX Audits and Stage 7 pre-ship gates.
